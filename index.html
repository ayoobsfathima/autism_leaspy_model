Disease Course Mapping for Autism — CARS2-ST Demonstration

The same multivariate ordinal disease course mapping method used to disentangle Parkinson's disease subtypes from MDS-UPDRS (Poulet & Durrleman, 2023, Statistics in Medicine) applied to CARS2-ST, a clinician-direct-observation autism severity scale. Fit on synthetic data (n=300) for demonstration purposes.

Prototype built on synthetic data, calibrated to match a published factor structure (Campbell et al., 2026) but not fit to real patients. Not for clinical decision-making. CARS2-ST item content is copyrighted (Schopler et al., 2010, WPS) and is not reproduced — items are shown only by number and their published short domain label.

Enter a child's visit history below (age + CARS2-ST item scores, 1–4 in 0.5 steps, grouped by Campbell et al.'s three published factors). The tool personalizes that child's developmental pace and timing on a shared disease-course timeline — the same mechanism used to personalize a Parkinson's patient's progression rate and onset, applied here to a developmental trajectory instead.

This is the direct analog of the original paper's Figure 5: correlating each item's individual-level space-shift across the population, then ordering items so correlated ones sit together. In Parkinson's, this revealed tremor-dominant vs. postural-instability/gait subtypes. Here, at n=300, it substantially recovers Campbell et al.'s (2026) published three-factor structure — Social Communication, Restrictive/Repetitive Behavior/Sensory, and Emotional/Behavioral Dysregulation — though imperfectly, since two of those factors (SC and RB) are published as highly correlated (r=.87) in the real instrument, making them inherently hard to fully separate.
